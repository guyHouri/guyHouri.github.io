Diabetes oxygen mechanismRead full source →
In diabetics, ISC (intersystem crossing) flips oxygen from paramagnetic to singlet (dimagnetic) state, preventing the RPE mitochondria from producing DDW (deuterium-depleted water) at CCO (cytochrome c oxidase). This electron backup between NAD+ and CCO creates a high-voltage electrical environment that dehydrates melanin in the retina. The cell's response is to downregulate insulin receptors - not because the pump is broken, but as a protective thermodynamic safety fuse to prevent further fuel entry into a system that cannot handle the electrical surge. Without the dielectric brake (metabolic water from CCO), accepting more glucose would trigger catastrophic oxidative collapse from singlet oxygen accumulation. This explains why blood glucose rises in blue light conditions alongside insulin resistance. Light environment and grounding status directly determine oxygen spin state and therefore insulin sensitivity.
ISC: Intersystem crossing: quantum transition between electron spin states, here from paramagnetic (unpaired electrons) to singlet (paired electrons) oxygen.
paramagnetic: Oxygen with unpaired electrons; can accept electrons and support aerobic metabolism; associated with coherence and entropy reduction.
singlet: Oxygen with paired electrons (dimagnetic); cannot accept electrons efficiently; associated with entropy increase and oxidative stress.
RPE: Retinal pigment epithelium: layer of cells behind the retina that supports photoreceptors and contains mitochondria critical for vision and glucose regulation.
DDW: Deuterium-depleted water: water with reduced deuterium content, produced at CCO and critical for mitochondrial function and coherence.
CCO: Cytochrome c oxidase: the final enzyme in the electron transport chain, containing copper atoms and responsible for reducing oxygen and producing metabolic water.
NAD+: Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide: electron carrier in the electron transport chain; electrons back up here when CCO cannot process them.
melanin: Pigment in the retina and skin; when dehydrated by singlet oxygen, loses its ability to support coherent electron flow and water ordering.
dielectric brake: Metabolic water produced at CCO that acts as an electrical insulator and coherence-builder, preventing electron runaway and oxidative collapse.

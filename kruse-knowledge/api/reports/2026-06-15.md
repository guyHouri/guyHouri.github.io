### Century-long North Atlantic cold anomaly linked to Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation weakening and South Atlantic Anomaly onset.

Tag: North Atlantic cold blob and geomagnetic field collapse

A forum post connects a persistent cold patch in the North Atlantic (south of Greenland) that has existed for over 100 years to long-term weakening of the AMOC (Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation), the massive ocean current system that regulates Northern Hemisphere climate. The new signal is a temporal correlation: the SAA (South Atlantic Anomaly) - a region of weakened Earth's geomagnetic field over the South Atlantic - arrived at the start of the 20th century, coinciding with the onset of the cold blob. This suggests that geomagnetic field degradation may disrupt ionospheric coupling or magnetohydrodynamic processes that regulate ocean circulation, making the magnetic field strength an active climate variable rather than a passive backdrop. The cold blob is not a transient weather event but a century-long signal of ocean circulation failure.

Source quote: "A new study links this anomaly to a long-term weakening of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC)"

- geomagnetic field (noob): Earth's magnetic field, generated by the planet's core; protects the atmosphere and influences biological and physical processes.

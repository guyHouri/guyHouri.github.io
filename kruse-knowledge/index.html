Lithium isotope brain disruptionRead full source →
Lithium enters the brain cell matrix as a subatomic disruption, introducing two stable isotopes - ^6Li with nuclear spin-1 and ^7Li with nuclear spin-3/2 - that have completely different quantum mechanical profiles. These mismatched isotopes create electromagnetic interference in the thalamus, the brain's internal FM radio receiver, jamming the universal 2 Hz neural integration frequency and decoupling circadian alignment to Earth's magnetic field. Lithium's high charge density alters the physical properties of cerebrospinal fluid and intracellular water, collapsing exclusion zone (EZ) water into chaotic bulk water and destroying the cell's primary deuterium sink. This structural collapse introduces foreign electronic states into the mitochondrial semiconductor bandgap, creating physical electron traps that halt coherent biophoton production at 160 THz and drive destructive singlet oxygen formation. Lithium naturally concentrates in the thyroid gland, disrupting iodination pathways and driving severe hypothyroidism.

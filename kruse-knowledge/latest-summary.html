Tag: Depression oxygen chemistry

Depression involves excessive deuterium bound to singlet oxygen, forming a bond 10 times stronger than the triplet oxygen-H+ bond. This stronger O-D coupling disrupts normal oxygen metabolism and explains why vitamin D deficiency commonly appears alongside depression. The mechanism frames depression not only as a nutrient, sunlight, and connection deficit, but as a biophysical problem rooted in deuterium load and singlet oxygen handling.

Concepts

singlet oxygen (pro)
An excited, high-energy form of oxygen that is more reactive than ground-state triplet oxygen; involved in oxidative stress and cellular signaling. When deuterium binds to singlet oxygen, the resulting O-D bond is 10 times stronger than the normal triplet oxygen-H+ bond, disrupting oxygen's normal metabolic role.
triplet oxygen (pro)
The ground-state form of molecular oxygen in air; lower energy and less reactive than singlet oxygen. The triplet oxygen-H+ bond is the normal, weaker interaction that allows oxygen to participate in healthy cellular metabolism.
vitamin D deficiency (noob)
Low circulating levels of vitamin D, a hormone-like molecule made in skin during sun exposure and involved in bone health, immune function, and mood regulation. According to this mechanism, deuterium-singlet oxygen coupling interferes with vitamin D production or metabolism, contributing to depression.
